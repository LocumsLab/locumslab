Section 127 Educational Assistance (S-Corp Benefit)

An S-Corp can provide up to $5,250/year per employee in tax-free educational assistance under IRC Section 127. This benefit is tax-deductible for the business and tax-free for the employee - covering tuition, books, fees, and (now permanently) student loan repayments.

✓ What's Covered
Tuition, books, fees, equipment, and student loan repayments (now permanent per OBBBA 2025). Limit indexed for inflation after 2026.
2% Shareholder Restriction
Solo S-Corp owners (2%+ shareholders) cannot use this benefit for themselves unless ≤5% of total costs go to such shareholders. Most beneficial if you have a W-2 employee (e.g., spouse).

Source: IRC §127 + One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, Pub. L. No. 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) - made student loan repayment permanent. Always consult your CPA to set up a written, non-discriminatory plan.

Mega Backdoor Roth (S-Corp Solo 401k)

S-Corp owners pay themselves a reasonable salary, which caps employer 401k contributions at 25% of that salary. This often means you can't hit the $70,000 annual limit through direct contributions alone. The Mega Backdoor Roth fills that gap.

Example: S-Corp CRNA, age 40, $120k reasonable salary
Employee contribution$23,500
Employer contribution (25% of $120k)$30,000
Direct contributions total$53,500
Gap to $70k limit$16,500 → Mega Backdoor Roth
How it works
Contribute the gap amount as "voluntary after-tax" (VAT) to your solo 401k, then immediately convert it to the Roth subaccount. All future growth is tax-free.
Requires a custom plan
Free plans at Fidelity, Schwab, and Vanguard do NOT support VAT contributions. You need a paid custom solo 401k (MySolo401k.net, Rocket Dollar, Ubiquity - ~$100–$300/yr). Offset by the $1,500 EACA tax credit (Form 8881).

Source: IRS §415(c), SECURE 2.0 Act. S-Corp solo 401k has 3 subaccounts: pretax, Roth, and voluntary after-tax (VAT). Consult your CPA before setting up.

S-Corp Strategy (BLS Safe Harbor)

We use your profession's BLS median salary as your W2 salary. This "safe harbor" approach is defensible to the IRS and maximizes distributions (which save 15.3% SE tax).
